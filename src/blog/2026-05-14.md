As a millennial, I was privileged to witness the transition from analog to digital. I still remember getting my first 256 MB MP3 player from Iriver as a gift from my aunt when I was in middle school. With 256 MB of storage, I could save around 60 songs on it.

I used to collect CDs from many of my favorite artists, but I never had a portable CD player. For me, having an MP3 player felt like such a luxury. I carefully curated the songs and renamed the files one by one. And I couldn’t have been happier because I could listen to my favorite music anywhere. It sounds so cheesy, but at the time, I truly believed my soul was connected to music.

{% figure "/images/26-05-14-01.jpg", "AltText" %}

However, I feel like I have lost that feeling in the world of streaming and algorithms. I passively listen to auto-generated playlists based on my listening history instead of actively discovering music myself. There was a time when, whenever someone asked me which artists I liked, I couldn’t answer, and I felt embarrassed. I always thought I knew what I liked, but it turned out that I didn’t.

So it was an eye-opening moment when I randomly came across Olivia Dean’s album [_Live At The Jazz Cafe_](https://music.apple.com/de/album/live-at-the-jazz-cafe/1589341424?l=en-GB) a few years ago. I fell in love with the album, and it became an inflection point that pushed me to explore what I actually like.

After missing the opportunity last year, I finally managed to get a ticket for Olivia Dean’s [_The Art of Loving_](https://music.apple.com/de/album/the-art-of-loving/1817609404?l=en-GB) tour in Berlin this week. I hadn’t been to a concert in such a long time, and it felt incredibly fulfilling.

{% figure "/images/26-05-14-02.jpeg", "AltText" %}
{% figure "/images/26-05-14-03.jpeg", "AltText" %}

One of the reasons I especially love her live albums is that she shares stories and background details about the songs. I never knew that [_Carmen_](https://music.apple.com/de/album/carmen/1676252821?i=1676253112&l=en-GB) was about her grandmother, who moved to the UK when she was nineteen, and I learned that during the concert.

Of course, she sang “Dive,” and it immediately brought me back to one of my dearest friends, S’s, wedding in Korea last year. She gave our friend group a hint that she would sing a song during the ceremony, but we had no idea which one it would be. Then it turned out to be “Dive.” It was truly a beautiful moment, and the song always brings me back to that memory. I feel like my soul is connected to music, not every day, but every now and then. And that makes me happy.
